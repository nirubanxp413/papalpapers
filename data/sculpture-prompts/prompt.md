# Sculpture prompt

## Project context

Translate a papal document’s meaning into one sculptural form: a **single recognizable object, religious symbol, devotional item, sacred figure, everyday object, or familiar archetypal character** that a text-to-image model can render clearly.

The final form must be **immediately legible** as something recognizable. It must not be primarily abstract, symbolic only through vague geometry, or dependent on explanation to be understood. The sculpture should embody the encyclical’s central argument by choosing the **one concrete subject** that best carries its meaning when isolated on its own.

Do **not** create an abstract sculpture under any circumstance. Even if the encyclical deals with mystery, paradox, transcendence, social systems, theology, ecology, economics, or spiritual interiority, the answer must still choose a recognizable subject rather than an abstract form.

Do **not** create a diorama, collage, assemblage, environment, tableau, symbolic cluster, or multi-object scene. Do **not** include text, inscriptions, lettering, or readable marks in the image.

The final image must have a **transparent background**. The object or figure should appear as an isolated high-end 3D render suitable for compositing, with no room, wall, landscape, horizon, floor, sky, or environmental background.

All generated images must look like **premium Houdini / Cinema 4D renders with Octane-style physically based rendering**: refined geometry, realistic material response, micro-surface detail, ray-traced reflections, pronounced ambient occlusion, cinematic contrast, and a luxury sacred-object finish.

## Selection rubric

Before writing the final concept and image prompt, choose the sculptural subject using this rubric:

1. **Recognizability first**  
Choose a subject that is instantly identifiable as a known object, figure, religious symbol, devotional item, or everyday thing. The viewer should not need an explanation to recognize what the object is.
2. **Single-subject discipline**  
Choose one coherent subject only. The sculpture may contain necessary built-in parts of that subject, such as a candle with flame, a shepherd with staff, or a chalice with stem and bowl, but it must not become a collection of separate symbols.
3. **Theological fit**  
Choose the subject that best expresses the encyclical’s core argument, moral claim, or spiritual emphasis. The subject should feel inevitable, not decorative.
4. **Concrete over abstract**  
Avoid abstraction completely. Do not use abstract geometry, amorphous forms, symbolic voids, conceptual shapes, floating fragments, or non-representational sculpture.
5. **Everyday or sacred legibility**  
Prefer forms drawn from religious practice, Catholic iconography, domestic life, labor, nature, care, pilgrimage, sacrifice, justice, nourishment, protection, or communal life.
6. **Isolation test**  
The subject must still communicate meaning when rendered alone on a transparent background, without scenery, supporting props, text, or a narrative context.
7. **Renderability test**  
Choose a subject that can be rendered clearly as a premium 3D sculpture with recognizable silhouette, readable material surfaces, and strong lighting.

Possible subject examples include: a shepherd, pilgrim, mother and child, saintly figure, angel, dove, lamb, chalice, monstrance, rosary, candle, lantern, key, crown of thorns, mitre, crozier, boat, anchor, door, heart, open hands, olive branch, loaf of bread, basin, vessel, scale, cross, fish, seed, tree sapling, cloak, bell, bridge, table, chair, well, bowl, lamp, broom, sandals, book, window, nest, broken chain, compass, shield, or walking staff.

These are **examples only**. Do **not** limit the answer to these examples. Choose whatever single recognizable subject best captures the encyclical’s core meaning.

## Task

From the summary above, write a brief concept and one text-to-image prompt for a **single unified recognizable sculptural subject**.

The chosen form must be something concrete and identifiable, such as:

- a religious object
- a devotional object
- a sacred symbol
- a liturgical object
- a familiar everyday object with strong symbolic meaning
- a single archetypal character or sacred figure
- a recognizable natural object
- a recognizable tool, vessel, garment, or domestic object

The sculpture may be stylized, monumental, sacred, cinematic, or materially elevated, but it must remain clearly recognizable as the chosen subject.

## Visual direction

### Camera

Use a precise dead-center product-render composition:

- orthographic or near-orthographic camera
- dead-center frontal view
- camera aimed directly at the midpoint of the object
- camera positioned at object mid-height
- 85mm lens equivalent
- aperture between f/8 and f/11
- symmetrical composition
- full object visible
- generous transparent negative space around the sculpture
- no tilt
- no Dutch angle
- no low angle
- no high angle
- no wide-angle distortion
- no dramatic perspective exaggeration

### Lighting

Use dramatic, high-contrast studio lighting:

- strong key light from above-front-left at roughly 45 degrees
- minimal fill light from front-right, only enough to preserve some material readability
- pronounced shadow falloff across the opposite side
- sharp or semi-hard rim light from rear-right to carve the silhouette
- chiaroscuro mood
- deep shadows
- strong highlights
- crisp specular definition
- pronounced ambient occlusion in recesses
- lighting should make the subject feel sacred, monumental, cinematic, and sculptural
- no flat lighting
- no soft commercial beauty lighting
- no evenly lit studio look
- no colored lighting unless absolutely necessary for material clarity

### Render style

Use:

- high-end Houdini / Cinema 4D / Octane-style render
- physically based materials
- global illumination
- ray-traced reflections
- realistic material response
- procedural microtexture
- subtle material imperfections
- refined sculptural geometry
- luxury sacred-object visualization
- transparent background
