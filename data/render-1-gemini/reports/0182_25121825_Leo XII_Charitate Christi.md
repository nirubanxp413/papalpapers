# Render: Charitate Christi

## Why

I chose a monstrance for this sculpture because it is the preeminent Catholic vessel for making the sacramental grace of Christ visibly present for adoration, perfectly embodying the encyclical's central theme: the extraordinary grace of the Jubilee is available, but its 'fruits depend entirely on the interior disposition of the faithful.' A monstrance is fundamentally a tool for presenting the sacred, yet its true purpose is realized only when we look at *what it holds*, mirroring the letter's call for interior renewal and proper sacramental participation (penance, communion) rather than passive celebration. I rejected abstraction because a non-representational form cannot capture the required theological specificity; a mere container is vague, but a monstrance is *instantly legible* as a sacred object designed to focus worship on the living presence of grace through the Sacrament. It stands alone as a complete devotional item, avoiding the need for a complex scene, multiple objects, or environments while powerfully summarizing the document’s manual for spiritual focus and pastoral oversight.
