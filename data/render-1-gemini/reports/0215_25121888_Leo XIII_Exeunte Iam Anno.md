# Render: Exeunte Iam Anno

## Why

"Exeunte Iam Anno" addresses a profound spiritual and moral crisis, tracing a clear path from "rationalism" and "materialism" to the decay of individual, family, and social life. Pope Leo XIII insists that the only remedy is a return to authentic Christian life, a persistent *struggle* against sin and concupiscence, and reliance on supernatural grace. A set of ancient, worn iron armor—not pristine, but heavily scarred and damaged by battles against invisible enemies—best symbolizes this lifelong moral combat. This single recognizable subject embodies the "good fight" and the active Christian resistance needed to oppose the errors of the age and restore order. I rejected abstraction (like geometric structures for "order" or symbols of "chaos") because the encyclical is not a conceptual description but a pastoral call to specific, active, concrete virtues and spiritual defense; the active nature of this struggle is best captured in a traditional, recognizable symbol of warfare. The damaged armor emphasizes that this fight is arduous, real, and leaves marks on the soul and body, yet the armor persists, reflecting the fortitude required to withstand "rationalism" and "materialism." A simple religious symbol (like a rosary) or a passive figure would fail to express the dimension of conflict against specific intellectual and moral decay described by the Pope.

This sculpture of a battle-scarred suit of armor translates the encyclical’s theme of moral struggle into a concrete, recognizable form. The single subject, positioned front-and-center, meets the requirement for immediate legibility, while the damaged texture effectively embodies the conflict against contemporary moral decay described by Pope Leo XIII. The composition and high-quality rendering successfully isolate the subject against the requested transparent background.
