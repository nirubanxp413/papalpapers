# Render: Traditi Humilitati

## Why

I chose a single, massive, fortified iron gate, barred and chained, as the sculptural subject because it powerfully visualizes Pope Pius VIII's urgent defense of the Church against a "cluster of interrelated dangers" (religious indifferentism, secret societies, and illicit translations). The encyclical describes the Church as "besieged" and "afflicted," demanding the strengthening of authority and barriers. A formidable gate immediately communicates a posture of defense, protection, and the rejection of external corruption, far more directly than any abstract form or collection of smaller symbols. By rendering it as a singular, monumental sculpture on a plinth, it satisfies the requirements for immediate recognition, theological fit, and single-subject discipline.
