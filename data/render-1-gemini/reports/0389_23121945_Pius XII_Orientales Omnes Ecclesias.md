# Render: Orientales Omnes Ecclesias

## Why

I chose a **liturgical vestment (Phelonion)** for the sculpture, specifically a heavy, gold-embroidered **Sticharion** and **Phelonion**, as the single recognizable subject. This Byzantine liturgical vestment immediately signals the Eastern identity and traditions of the Ruthenian Church, which Pope Pius XII emphasizes and defends in *Orientales Omnes Ecclesias*. The central theological and historical tension of the document is the defense of the Ruthenian faithful and their distinct Eastern rite against both forced Latinization and Soviet absorption into the Russian Orthodox Church. A stylized Eastern vestment, rendered as a monumental, rigid sculpture, acts as a concrete embodiment of this "protected identity" and historical continuity with Rome; it's an object of sacred devotion that was the primary point of contention and suppression. I rejected abstraction because it could not legibly convey the specific historical and ritual context of the Ruthenian Union. I also rejected broader symbols like a mitre or cross, which do not speak to the *specific* defense of the Eastern rite's disciplines and traditions highlighted in the commemorative letter. The single vestment, appearing almost as a "witness" of faith, fulfills the requirement for a single, recognizable subject with powerful theological weight and immediate legibility.
