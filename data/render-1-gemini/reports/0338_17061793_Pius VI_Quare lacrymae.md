# Render: Quare lacrymae

## Why

I chose a single, stylized, martyr's crown of thorns integrated with a shattered royal crown. This form directly visualizes Pope Pius VI's central theological and political argument in *Quare lacrymae*: that Louis XVI was not merely a executed king but a true martyr for the Catholic faith. By fusing the iconic religious symbol of suffering for the faith with the recognizable sign of sovereignty, the sculpture embodies the pope's claim of martyrdom by analogy with Mary Queen of Scots and his condemnation of the regicide driven by anti-Catholic hatred. This concrete imagery was chosen over abstract representations of grief or political upheaval to ensure immediate legibility and to visually elevate Louis’s suffering to a sacred status, as argued in the document. An abstract form would fail to communicate the explicit, specific, and dual nature of the event as defined by the pontiff.
