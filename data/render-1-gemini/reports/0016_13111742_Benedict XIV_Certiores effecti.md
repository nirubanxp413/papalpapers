# Render: Certiores effecti

## Why

The most direct embodiment of Benedict XIV’s direction in *Certiores effecti* is the pyx, the specific vessel used to carry the Eucharist to those who cannot attend Mass, or to distribute Communion *outside* the context of the main celebration. It represents the very act of bringing the Sacrament to the faithful who present themselves ready to receive, separate from the priest’s own private reception.

The pyx is a recognizable sacred object that avoids abstraction, clearly containing and presenting the Host. The act of bringing the pyx to the communicant visualizes the Pope’s primary directive, navigating the complex theological territory by focusing on the moment of delivery and orderly access to the Sacrament. Other subjects, like a chalice, focus too much on the sacrifice of the Mass itself, rather than the distribution of Communion to the faithful, which is the specific controversy addressed. A simple ciborium is more static and communal than the targeted action represented by the pyx. The pyx, as an object defined by its purpose of transporting the Eucharist *to* the recipient, perfectly isolates and materializes the core argument of the document: that the faithful have a right to receive when properly disposed, distinct from the validity of private Masses. A sculpture of an empty hand or open arms would be too abstract and less clearly connected to the Sacramental context.
