# Render: Letter to the Bishops, Priests, Consecrated Persons and the Lay Faithful in the People's Republic of China

## Why

To represent the fractured situation and the call for unity in Benedict XVI’s letter to China, I have chosen a specific sacred item: a fragmented and rejoined chalice. The chalice is the essential object of Catholic communion, but its state here—fractured but meticulously bonded together with a gold repair (reminiscent of the *Kintsugi* technique)—best captures the letter’s central themes. This form visually embodies the division of the Church into open and underground factions, the wounds of division, and the profound necessity of achieving true, unified communion (Koinonia) through dialogue and reconciliation, as emphasized by the Pope. The singular, recognizable form of the chalice was chosen over abstraction because it immediately grounds the letter's theological and practical concerns in a tangible liturgical object, symbolizing a community that is broken but fundamentally aimed toward re-establishment and the Eucharist, which abstraction cannot convey.
