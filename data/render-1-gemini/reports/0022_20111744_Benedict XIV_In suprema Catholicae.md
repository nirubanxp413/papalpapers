# Render: In suprema Catholicae

## Why

I chose a monumental, broken Thurible as the single recognizable sculptural form for *In suprema Catholicae*. This sacred object, used to offer incense representing prayer, visually encapsulates Pope Benedict XIV's initial call for prayer and liturgical processions (like the solemn procession decreed) as the response to divine wrath and societal collapse. By rendering the Thurible as *broken*—fractured and silent—it perfectly embodies the central conflict: the shattering of the Pope’s hopes for peace, the "evident signs of divine indignation," and the chaotic reality of kingdoms at war, plague, and devastation which interrupted his plans. A whole, beautiful Thurible would suggest a successful, peaceful petition, whereas a broken one captures the crisis, the perceived rupture in divine favor, and the immediate need for penitential acts like the Jubilee to restore balance, rejecting abstract symbols in favor of this tangible, recognizable liturgical tool and its dramatic narrative.
