# Render: Quae Honorem Conditoris Omnium

## Why

A single, massive helmet, the 'sallet of salvation,' best embodies Innocent IV's ratification of the Carmelite Rule, as the document's central theological motif is spiritual warfare, commanding the brothers to put on the 'armour of God.' This concrete, recognisable object immediately communicates the disciplined, warrior-like dedication required by the rule, rejecting abstraction which would fail to capture the militant vigilance and specific, detailed demands for spiritual battle. The helmet stands as the single, governing metaphor, visually representing the protective virtues—the guard of the senses and intellect—which the detailed observances (fasting, silence, prayer) are designed to cultivate in the Carmelite hermit.
