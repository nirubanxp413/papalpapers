# Render: Ad Apostolorum Principis

## Why

To represent Pope Pius XII’s *Ad Apostolorum Principis*, which defends papal authority in China against state-controlled bishop appointments, a single, monumental Papal Mitre was chosen as the sculptural subject. The mitre is an instantly recognizable, concrete symbol of the Bishop of Rome's legitimate authority and universal jurisdiction, which is the central theological claim of the encyclical. A mitre provides a singular, coherent form that embodies ecclesiastical office and the power of order and jurisdiction, effectively summarizing the document’s focus on the 'one true Church' without resorting to abstract geometry or a complex scene. Other symbols, like broken chains (too narrative) or a shepherd (less specific to the jurisdictional conflict), were set aside for the Mitre's direct, hierarchical clarity in a single form.
