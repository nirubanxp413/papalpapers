# Render: Vita Consecrata

## Why

I chose a single, magnificent, three-branch candelabrum (a trinitarian *candelabrum trinitatis*) as the sculptural subject for *Vita Consecrata*. The document’s central theological core, drawing on the Transfiguration, presents consecrated life as a living confession of the Trinity (*confessio Trinitatis*), where the three evangelical counsels (poverty, chastity, obedience) reflect the inner life of the divine and configure the individual to Christ.

A candelabrum with three branches, holding three actively burning flames, perfectly embodies this "confession of light." It is immediately recognizable as a sacred liturgical and domestic object, satisfying the legibility requirement. The unified base represents the singular life of consecration, while the three separate yet unified branches and their light clearly symbolize the Trinity and the three distinct yet harmonious vows. The burning flames capture the "fire" of charity, prayer, and apostolic mission described throughout the text. I rejected abstract forms because a geometric sculpture of "three-in-one" would lack the spiritual warmth, theological specificty, and recognized devotional resonance required. A literal candlestick provides the necessary concrete legibility and sacred dignity to anchor the encyclical's profound spiritual message.
